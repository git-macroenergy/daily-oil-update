## Trump Says Russia and Ukraine Agreed to an Energy Truce — Zelensky Says It's Still Just a Proposal

<cite index="55-6">Trump said Russia and Ukraine had agreed not to strike each other's energy infrastructure</cite>, but Zelensky's own position is conditional: <cite index="55-2">Kyiv is ready to halt strikes only if partners can ensure Russia genuinely refrains from striking Ukraine's electricity system and other critical infrastructure</cite>. Kremlin spokesman Peskov said <cite index="54-4">avoiding attacks on civilian economic infrastructure can certainly only be welcomed</cite>, without committing Moscow to reciprocate. <cite index="26-8">Hours before Trump's remarks, Ukraine's military struck an oil refinery in Russia's Tatarstan republic</cite>. Until Moscow puts reciprocity in writing, this is a headline that dents the diesel crack for a session, not a new price floor.

## A Three-Hour Power Blip Just Exposed How Thin Chicago's Fuel Supply Really Is

<cite index="44-1">A power outage knocked ExxonMobil's Channahon refinery offline starting around 3:30 p.m. Sunday for roughly three and a half hours</cite>, and <cite index="44-9">workers were still conducting a site status assessment as of Monday</cite> with no restart timeline given. <cite index="44-3">The refinery processes up to 275,000 barrels of crude daily and supplies finished fuel to seven Midwestern states</cite>, and <cite index="44-6">the Chicagoland fuel market leans on just three refineries — Joliet, BP Whiting, and Citgo Lemont — representing 909,000 b/d combined</cite>. A few hours is a non-event; the number to watch is how fast Exxon confirms full rates, given this plant's history of multi-week outages.

## Update: Saudi Arabia's Yanbu Buffer Now Has a Five-Day Countdown Clock

Update on the East-West pipeline shutdown: <cite index="65-2">Yanbu has enough stored crude to maintain current export levels for roughly five to seven days without new supplies flowing through the pipeline</cite>, and <cite index="66-8">repair estimates range from a faster partial resumption to five-to-six weeks fully offline</cite>. What's changed: <cite index="110-4">Houthi forces have captured the Hanish islands, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a key passage for Saudi oil shipments now serving as an alternative to Hormuz</cite>, and <cite index="104-4">regional officials say the pipeline will mostly be out of service for weeks</cite>. If repairs land on the slow end, <cite index="66-3">the world risks losing as much as 4% of its oil supply</cite>.
