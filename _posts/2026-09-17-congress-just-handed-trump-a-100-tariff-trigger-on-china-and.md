## Congress Just Handed Trump a 100% Tariff Trigger on China and India's Oil Imports — He Hasn't Pulled It Yet

The mechanism now exists to tariff Beijing and New Delhi at up to 100% for buying Russian barrels — Congress passed it, Trump is expected to sign within days. But the law hands him discretion, not a mandate, meaning the real market-moving event is still ahead: whether he pulls the trigger or holds it as leverage. India has already warned the move could rupture ties, and China called it illegal long-arm jurisdiction. If enforced, expect a scramble for non-Russian barrels that tightens Middle East and Atlantic Basin grades overnight.

## Goldman Just Abandoned Its Own Diesel Trade — The Real Squeeze Is Moving to Gasoline

Update: after months of calling diesel the epicenter of the fuel crunch, Goldman flipped its trading recommendation to European gasoline for mid-2027 — not because diesel is easing, but because refiners chasing diesel margins are starving gasoline output. That's a distinct, new mechanical risk: the product squeeze is spreading sideways instead of resolving. With U.S. diesel already at record highs and gasoline trailing, any fresh refinery outage now hits two tight products instead of one.

## Harold Hamm Just Bet on 30 Billion Barrels He'll Never Own — And Won't Touch for 18 Months

Continental's MOU with PDVSA sounds like a supply game-changer — 30 billion barrels, 100% working interest — but the fine print says otherwise: that figure is oil-in-place, not recoverable reserves, primary recovery in the Orinoco typically nets single digits to low teens percent, and under Venezuela's production-sharing structure Continental will own none of the barrels in the ground. First oil is targeted around 18 months out, contingent on a still-unsigned long-term contract. This is a bet on future access and political durability, not a near-term supply fix — Chevron's brownfield growth remains the barrels that actually move 2026 output.
