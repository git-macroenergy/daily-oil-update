## A Weak Tropical Storm Just Found the One Crack in America's Refining System

Tropical Storm Edouard was <cite index="118-10">not expected to reach hurricane strength</cite>, yet it still forced a shutdown of one crude unit at Valero's 385,000-bpd Port Arthur refinery while <cite index="121-4,121-6">Motiva's 656,400-bpd complex next door and Exxon's Beaumont refinery kept running normally</cite>. That containment matters less than the timing: it hit the same week <cite index="97-7">national refinery utilization reached 97.4% of 18.03 million bpd</cite> and <cite index="107-1,107-4">GasBuddy's Labor Day forecast broke the previous nominal record of $3.83 a gallon</cite>. Zero spare capacity means the next storm doesn't need to be bigger — it just needs to hit the wrong refinery.

## Russia's Own Budget Numbers Just Wrote Off Any Oil Recovery Before 2030

Moscow's draft budget, obtained by Reuters, <cite index="63-1,63-2">cut its 2026-2029 crude output forecasts by 16 to 20 million tons versus the estimate published in May</cite>, with the baseline showing <cite index="63-5">crude production falling 17.2 million tons this year to 494.2 million tons</cite> — <cite index="67-6">9.88 million barrels a day, the lowest since 2009</cite>. <cite index="63-7">Fuel exports are projected to drop 27.3 million tons this year</cite>. This isn't a refinery-outage story; it's the Kremlin's own numbers conceding the war has permanently shrunk its production base. Anyone modeling a Russian supply rebound once fighting cools should push that timeline well past 2029.

## India Just Put a Windfall Tax Back on Petrol Exports for the First Time Since Spring

New Delhi <cite index="53-6">hiked the windfall levy on petrol exports to Rs 1.5 per litre effective September 1, up from zero</cite>, while also <cite index="53-4">raising the diesel export levy to Rs 25 per litre from Rs 24</cite>. The finance ministry's own rationale is the tell: <cite index="55-9,55-10">the tax exists to increase domestic fuel availability and stop exporters taking undue advantage of price differences since global crude oil prices rose amid the war</cite>. Putting a levy back on petrol while Washington and Tehran trade fresh strikes suggests India isn't betting on this war, or the price gap it created, closing anytime soon.
