## Saudi Arabia's Hormuz Escape Hatch Just Shut Itself — The East-West Pipeline Is Now Offline Too

Saudi Arabia's only crude export route that never touched Hormuz just went dark. Drones launched from Iraq hit pump stations on the 7-million-b/d East-West pipeline Thursday, forcing a precautionary shutdown of the line that <cite index="25-12">carries Saudi crude from Abqaiq to Yanbu on the Red Sea, which is Saudi Arabia's only export route that does not pass through the Strait of Hormuz</cite>. With Hormuz choked and Houthis holding Bab el-Mandeb, the kingdom now has zero working bypass — every barrel is exposed.

## Six Gulf Foreign Ministers Are Flying to Oman Monday — the Meeting Is Already Priced Into $100 Oil

This is an escalation of the Iran-Oman shipping talks, not a repeat: Oman has now pulled in all six GCC foreign ministers plus Iraq for a Monday sit-down with Tehran in Salalah, and crude already sold off on the news. <cite index="31-3">Gulf foreign ministers plan to meet their Iranian counterpart in a push by Oman and Iran to secure buy-in for a temporary deal to manage shipping through the Strait of Hormuz</cite>. Buy-in isn't a ceasefire — if Monday underdelivers, the $15 gap that just opened between hope and reality snaps shut fast.

## The IEA Just Called a 2.5 Million-Barrel Demand Collapse — And Refining Margins Hit a Record Anyway

Falling demand was supposed to be the pressure release valve — instead it's arriving alongside record refiner profits, which means this squeeze isn't a demand story at all. The IEA is <cite index="3-1,3-2">forecasting a 2.5 million-barrel-a-day contraction in 2026, the largest annual decline since the Covid-19 pandemic, as higher prices and tighter supplies weigh on consumption, while OPEC cut its 2026 demand-growth forecast for a fifth consecutive time</cite>. Meanwhile <cite index="12-3,12-4,12-5">the rise in refined products has pushed diesel above $200/bbl in the US, 94% above pre-war levels, pushing refinery margins to record levels in the Atlantic Basin</cite>. Demand destruction won't fix a capacity problem.
