## Iran Signs the Hormuz Traffic Deal Monday — Then Confirms the Strait Stays Closed Anyway

Update: the Iran-Oman corridor framework we flagged September 7 now has a signing date — Monday in Muscat, with routes to be briefed to other Gulf states. But Tehran got specific about what it isn't: an Iranian source told Tasnim the arrangement covers entry and exit routes only, and <cite index="12-11,12-12">the agreement does not amount to an immediate reopening of the strait, with Hormuz remaining closed until Tehran's conditions involving the US are met.</cite> A vessel was struck near Qeshm Island hours before the signing. Traffic is still <cite index="20-8">about 90% below pre-conflict levels</cite>. Paperwork isn't barrels.

## The US Government's Own Model Says Oil Falls to $77 by Spring — Nobody's Trading Like They Believe It

Buried in the September STEO is a forecast the market is ignoring: EIA sees <cite index="59-1,59-2,59-3">Brent decreasing to an average of $77/b by 2Q27, with most shut-in production largely restored in 2H27 and inventories building again, gradually lowering oil prices to an average of $67/b in 2H27</cite>. The premise is a new UAE bypass pipeline and <cite index="62-8">shipping companies continuing to find workarounds using pipeline and overland bypass routes and increased ship-to-ship transfers</cite>. If Monday's Muscat signing stalls or Houthi attacks widen, that curve breaks — and it breaks toward $120, not $90.

## Diesel Just Broke Its All-Time Price Record With Crude $28 Cheaper Than Last Time — And Goldman Says the Margin Stays That Way Into 2027

US diesel hit <cite index="31-2">an all-time record of $5.901 per gallon on 8 September 2026, surpassing the previous June 2022 peak despite crude trading roughly $28 per barrel cheaper</cite>. That's not a crude story — the ULSD crack hit $108/bbl versus $64-73 in 2022. The part that should worry planners: <cite index="31-5">Goldman Sachs forecasts U.S. diesel refining profits at $63 per barrel extending into 2027, with European jet fuel bans on Russian supply running through November setting a structural floor on margins</cite>. This isn't a spike to trade through; it's a new cost baseline for trucking, ag, and freight.
