## Futures Are Falling, But Physical Crude Just Hit a $20 Premium — That's the Number That Matters

Brent futures just logged a third straight losing session, sliding toward $103 on hopes the Saudi pipeline crisis is easing. Don't trust the screen. Physical Dated Brent cargoes are trading at more than a $20/bbl premium to those same front-month futures, a gap Energy Intelligence says shows no relief in sight. Futures reflect sentiment about tomorrow's supply; Dated Brent reflects what refiners are actually paying to get barrels today. Until that spread narrows, the crunch hasn't eased — it's just gone quiet on the ticker.

## Russia Just Pushed Its Diesel Export Ban Into November — And Killed Europe's Backdoor on the Same Week

Moscow just extended its producer diesel export ban through October 31, an update from the September 30 cutoff markets had priced in, as Ukrainian drone strikes keep knocking Russian refineries offline. The timing compounds the pain: the EU's 18th sanctions package now blocks refined products made from Russian crude routed through India, Turkey, and China — the workaround Europe had leaned on. S&P estimates that alone removes 250,000 b/d of diesel. Layer a crippled Saudi pipeline on top and Europe is short crude, short the Russian back door, and short its own refining capacity at once.

## Everyone's Watching Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb — the Houthis Are Actually Marching on Yemen's Oil Fields

Every headline this month has framed Yemen as a shipping-lane story — tankers, straits, insurance. The bigger risk is inland: Houthi forces are pushing toward Marib, Yemen's main oil and gas province, in fighting analysts call a potential watershed moment for the war. This isn't about disrupting transit anymore; it's about a hostile, Iran-aligned militia physically capturing producing fields. Saudi Arabia's most important ally, the U.S., is sitting this one out, leaving government-backed forces as the only thing standing between the Houthis and the wellheads.
