## US Crude Stocks Just Jumped 17.4 Million Barrels — It's an Export Bottleneck, Not a Supply Glut

Don't read this as the Hormuz crisis easing. EIA data show <cite index="39-1,39-2">US crude oil inventories unexpectedly surged by 17.4 million barrels last week, the largest gain since January 2023, due to a drop in exports. Gasoline stocks fell less than expected, while refinery utilization dipped.</cite> Barrels aren't finding buyers overseas, so they're piling up onshore while Brent stays elevated. Expect the WTI-Brent spread to keep widening and Cushing's refill to remove a domestic price prop — a logistics story dressed up as a bearish supply signal.

## OPEC+ Says It Finished Unwinding Cuts. The War Says Those Barrels Never Left the Ground

Headlines will say OPEC+ just completed its cut rollback with a sixth straight monthly hike — <cite index="17-1">OPEC+ has agreed to raise oil production quotas for the sixth consecutive month by 188,000 barrels per day in September, completing the phased rollback</cite>. But <cite index="17-3,17-4">export disruptions affecting Gulf producers, Russia, and Kazakhstan amid regional conflicts have meant much of the additional supply approved this year has yet to reach the market</cite>. Treat every future 'OPEC+ raises output' headline as a paper event until seaborne loadings confirm it — the cap moved, the flow didn't.

## Refiners Chased Jet Fuel Margins for Months. Diesel Is Now Collecting the Bill

The diesel squeeze building into fall didn't start this week — it started when refiners pivoted. <cite index="31-6,31-9">Shell increased its jet fuel production by approximately 20% in response to price signals, according to CEO Wael Sawan's remarks; more jet fuel output means less diesel and gasoline from the same barrel of crude.</cite> Now <cite index="31-10">as the jet fuel shortage eased and aviation activity normalized, the diesel and gasoline deficit that accumulated during the pivot is showing up in product-market signals</cite>. That deferred deficit lands just as heating-oil buying starts — diesel tightness gets worse before it gets better.
