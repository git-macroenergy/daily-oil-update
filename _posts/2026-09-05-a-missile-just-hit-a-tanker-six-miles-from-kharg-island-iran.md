## A Missile Just Hit a Tanker Six Miles From Kharg Island — Iran's Only Untouched Oil Lifeline Just Got Closer to the Line

For six months, Washington has drawn a hard line around Kharg Island's terminal itself, even while bombing its defenses. Iranian state media reported <cite index="108-1,106-11">missiles struck a tanker about six miles (10 kilometers) from Kharg Island, home to a terminal through which the country exports most of its oil</cite>. That's the closest the war has come to the actual loading jetties CSIS estimated could add <cite index="143-3,143-5">roughly $10 per barrel, with prices surging above $100</cite> if disabled. If this expands from tankers to terminal, the market repricing happens before anyone confirms damage.

## Iraq Just Doubled Its Hormuz Oil Flows — By Getting Iran's Own Blessing to Pass and Discounting the Barrels

While the market fixates on tanker attacks, Iraq quietly rebuilt its export flow through the same strait. <cite index="20-5">Iraq more than doubled its crude oil exports via the Strait of Hormuz in August as it offered mouth-watering discounts for loadings at Basrah and Iran allowed Iraqi oil cargoes to transit the chokepoint</cite>. Basra loadings climbed from <cite index="21-9,21-10">an average of 1.9 million bpd by August 26, up sharply from about 100,000 bpd in May</cite>. Discounted Basra barrels are now the pressure valve capping the war premium other Gulf grades try to charge — complicating OPEC+'s Q4 output math.

## Dangote's $1.5 Billion Refinery IPO Opens in 9 Days — Priced for a War Premium That Might Not Last

Africa's biggest refinery is going public at the exact moment Gulf disruption is inflating its margins. <cite index="51-1,51-5">Dangote Group is planning to price the IPO at 525 naira per share, potentially raising about $1.5 billion, with the order book opening September 14</cite>, and <cite index="47-7">the refining industry has benefited from stronger profits as turmoil in the Middle East boosted demand for alternative fuel supplies</cite>. The pitch to investors bakes in elevated crack spreads and jet fuel scarcity as durable. A Hormuz de-escalation compresses exactly the margin story the roadshow is selling.
