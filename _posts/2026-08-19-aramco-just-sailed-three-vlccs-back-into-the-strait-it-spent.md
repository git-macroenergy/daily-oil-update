## Aramco Just Sailed Three VLCCs Back Into the Strait It Spent Weeks Avoiding

Aramco just reversed its own workaround. After weeks of routing cargoes around Hormuz, three VLCCs loaded directly from Ras Tanura and Juaymah between Aug. 12-16, ending a three-week gap, with six more tankers queued to follow this month. The read: Riyadh now judges Hormuz risk as more manageable than the alternative — a Houthi-blockaded Red Sea route Asian buyers are refusing to sail. Expect heavier-grade crude and residue-fuel supply to loosen first, with Saudi-flagged Bahri tankers testing the strait next.

## Iran's Hormuz Toll Booth Just Got Its First Fatal Enforcement Action

Within hours of the US-Iran 60-day deal lapsing, Iran detained a UAE-managed tanker near Qeshm for not paying transit fees, and a separate bulker was struck exiting the strait, killing its chief engineer. This isn't random escalation — it's toll enforcement. Lloyd's already voids war-risk cover for any ship that pays Iran's fee, so operators now face a binary trap: pay Tehran and lose insurance, or refuse and risk exactly this outcome. That combination, not diplomacy, is what's actually rationing Hormuz traffic.

## UPDATE: Jazan Just Took a Third Hit — What Changed Since the Second Strike

Update to the Aug. 12 Jazan story: Houthis claimed a third drone strike on the 400,000 bpd refinery on Aug. 18, roughly two weeks after the second attack that already pushed the restart to Aug. 30. What's new is the pattern — this is now three claimed hits in under three weeks on a plant that's been offline since late July, each one resetting the inspection clock before repair crews finish. The restart date is becoming a moving target, not a fixed one, with real consequences for an already-strained diesel market.
