## ADNOC Just Became Iraq's Oil Lifeline — And Iran Immediately Punished It For That

ADNOC's dark-transit shuttle system has quietly become the workaround keeping Gulf barrels moving, and this week it started extending that service to Iraqi crude, whose exports jumped to roughly 2 million b/d on the strength of steep discounts. Within a day, two ADNOC-linked tankers running the same route were struck by drones, bringing total attacks on its fleet to 18. If Iran is now targeting the shuttle mechanism itself rather than just individual flags, the one improvised logistics fix propping up non-Iranian Gulf exports — and Iraq's fiscal survival — is the next thing at risk of going dark.

## Bessent's 'Economic Isolation' Pivot Just Told Oil Markets: Stop Waiting for a Hormuz Deal

Treasury's Bessent promised measures amounting to economic isolation "like the world has never seen before," paired with what Defense Secretary Hegseth called an indefinite naval blockade of Iranian ports. That's a different signal than prior rounds of reparations talk or reopening rumors — it tells markets Washington has shelved the reopening-deal timeline in favor of a prolonged siege. Cracks and diesel have been trading partly on hope that Iran-Oman talks resolve Hormuz soon; an indefinite blockade removes that catalyst entirely, meaning the current price floor isn't a peak to fade but a baseline to plan around.

## Diesel Just Hit a Record No August Has Ever Seen — And a Hormuz Deal Won't Undo It

National gas hit $4.03 and diesel $5.40 this week — seasonal records that have never happened this late in August. With U.S. refineries already running near 96.5% utilization, the constraint increasingly isn't crude availability, it's capacity: lost refining runs from closures don't come back even if Hormuz reopens tomorrow. That's the case for treating current product prices as a structural floor, not a war premium — anyone modeling a snapback on a Hormuz resolution is pricing the wrong variable.
