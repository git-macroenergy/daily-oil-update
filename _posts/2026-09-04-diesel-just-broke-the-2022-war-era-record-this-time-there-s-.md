## Diesel Just Broke the 2022 War-Era Record — This Time There's No Single Villain to Blame

This is an update, not a repeat: after weeks of diesel *margins* setting records, the retail pump price itself just overtook the June 2022 all-time high. <cite index="32-8,32-9">The price of diesel fuel hit a record Friday as the wars in Ukraine and Iran knock out refineries, triggering a global supply crunch, with truckers paying an average of $5.85 a gallon nationwide, a nearly 60% increase over the same period last year</cite>. <cite index="32-6,32-7">About 8% of global diesel demand is disrupted right now, with Russia's export ban cutting 800,000 bpd and Hormuz disruptions removing 1.2 million bpd</cite>. Expect freight and food-price pass-through before this reverses.

## Israel's Last Restraint on Striking Iran's Oil Facilities Just Got a Public Expiration Date

The one thing that's kept Iran's oil infrastructure off the target list all year was a US veto — that veto now has a trigger condition attached. <cite index="48-2">Israel's Defence Minister said on Thursday that Israel would strike Iran's energy and civilian infrastructure if Iran were to attack Israel</cite>, days after <cite index="48-3">the U.S. and Iran exchanged their biggest barrage since July, with Washington striking Iran's southern coast and Tehran firing at U.S. bases across the region</cite>. If Iran hits Israeli territory, the guardrail on Iranian crude and gas export capacity disappears with it.

## Georgia's Only Refinery Just Finished Ditching Russian Crude — the EU's Sanctions Playbook Actually Worked

A six-month sanctions grace period just produced exactly the compliance Brussels wanted, and it's a template other third-country refiners processing Russian barrels should expect to face next. <cite index="72-3,72-4">Black Sea Petroleum's Kulevi refinery has been processing exclusively non-Russian feedstock, successfully completing its switch away from Russian-origin crude oil, following a transition period during July and the first three weeks of August</cite>, replacing it with Kazakh barrels and <cite index="72-5,72-6">a first shipment of Libyan crude oil delivered August 30 under a long-term agreement</cite>. Higher logistics costs are now baked into its margins permanently.
