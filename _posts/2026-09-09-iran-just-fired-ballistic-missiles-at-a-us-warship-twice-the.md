## Iran Just Fired Ballistic Missiles at a US Warship Twice — Then Hit Jordan With Real Missiles, Not Threats

CENTCOM's tanker-for-warship exchange rate just repriced again: after the <cite index="18-1">Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targeted an American Navy warship with ballistic missiles twice over a 48-hour period</cite>, the US <cite index="19-5">carried out coordinated strikes against five vessels that CENTCOM described as belonging to Iran's state-linked maritime fleet</cite>, up from three after the last warship attempt. Iran didn't just threaten Jordan this time — its Guard <cite index="18-8">targeted the US-operated Al-Azraq airbase in Jordan using liquid- and solid-fuel ballistic missiles</cite>, while separately <cite index="25-4">claiming ballistic missile attacks on the US Navy DDG-119 and DDG-53 destroyers</cite>. The exchange is escalating in both scale and geography.

## The US Just Ran Its Emergency Oil Reserve Down to 1982 Levels — Right as Brent Clears $100

Washington's insurance policy against exactly this scenario — Brent above $100 mid-war — has been running dry all year. <cite index="31-1">Stocks of crude oil in the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell by about 1.2 million barrels to 285.4 million barrels last week, the lowest level since November 1982</cite>. That's <cite index="32-10">only about 41% of the reserve's authorized storage capacity of approximately 714 million barrels</cite>. If Hormuz flows tighten further, the next emergency release draws from a reserve with far less cushion than it had when this conflict started.

## China's Teapot Refiners Are About to Cut Runs — The Iran-Venezuela Squeeze Finally Caught Up

China's independent refiners built their cost edge on discounted Iranian and Venezuelan barrels — and both taps are tightening together. <cite index="45-1,45-2">China's private oil refiners may cut processing runs in the coming weeks as traditional sources of crude supply become scarcer and costs surge, potentially hurting demand in the biggest importer</cite>, because <cite index="45-3">shipments from both Iran and Venezuela are under pressure from a US naval blockade against Tehran's ports and redirection of South American flows following Washington's intervention</cite>. Throttled teapot runs would pull the one swing supply of cheap diesel that's been easing global product markets — right as crack spreads sit at records.
