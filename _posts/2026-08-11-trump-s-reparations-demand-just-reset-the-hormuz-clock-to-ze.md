## Trump's Reparations Demand Just Reset the Hormuz Clock to Zero

A Hormuz reopening once framed as imminent now resets to square one. Trump has demanded Iran compensate the US for people it has killed in conflicts, after Tehran itself pressed for reparations — a swap of maximalist asks neither side can concede quickly. Expect the Iran-Oman shipping-route talks to keep progressing technically while the US-Iran track that actually unlocks it stalls. Brent's four-day climb toward $88 isn't pricing a deal anymore; it's pricing indefinite delay dressed as diplomacy.

## Aramco Calls Red Sea Strikes 'No Material Impact.' Its Tankers Are Now Sailing Dark.

Corporate messaging and fleet behavior are diverging fast. Aramco's chief has said recent facility attacks caused only brief interruptions with no material operational or financial impact — yet the Jazan refinery has now been hit twice in two weeks, and Saudi-flagged tankers are running dark through the Red Sea after a third claimed Houthi strike. When operators stop broadcasting AIS signals, that's risk management, not confidence. Watch OSP cuts and rerouting costs for the real read on damage, not the press statements.

## US Refiners Skipped Spring Maintenance. Hurricane Season Is About to Send the Bill.

Running near 95% utilization while postponing turnarounds works fine — until it doesn't. US refiners have deferred maintenance through H1 to chase strong margins, with shutdowns averaging roughly a third of last year's pace. That bet now overlaps with the Atlantic's historical peak window for hurricane formation, concentrated on a Gulf Coast holding the bulk of PADD 3 capacity. A below-normal storm forecast doesn't retire the tail risk: one landfall on an already-strained, maintenance-light system does more damage than a busy season hitting fresh-turnaround plants.
