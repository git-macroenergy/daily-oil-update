## Update: Aramco Didn't Just Trim Europe's September Cargoes — It Just Zeroed Out All of October

What started September 16 as scattered cargo delays has escalated fast: <cite index="109-1,109-3,109-4">Saudi Aramco told at least two European refining customers they will be allocated no crude next month under long-term agreements, with the decision applying to all European buyers</cite>. Poland's Orlen isn't waiting to find out how bad it gets — <cite index="127-1">it has issued more than ten tenders since last Friday to secure alternative supplies</cite>, since <cite index="126-1">Aramco delivers roughly 40% of the crude Orlen processes</cite>. If repairs run the full six weeks, expect this replacement-barrel scramble to define European refining margins through November.

## Update: Exxon's Joliet 'Three-Hour Blip' Just Became a Five-Day Blackout — Diesel Broke Its Record Again

The Sunday power trip we flagged as a short-lived stress test wasn't the whole story. <cite index="91-3">A Thursday filing disclosed that floodwater had overwhelmed a pump at the plant</cite>, and <cite index="87-2">the refinery has remained offline through Thursday</cite>, five days after the initial outage. The consequence is already visible at the pump: <cite index="91-8">nationwide diesel prices have jumped to a record $6.45 a gallon</cite>, with <cite index="87-9">Michigan diesel up 66 cents week-over-week</cite>. Restart timing, not the original outage, is now the number that moves Midwest fuel prices.

## US Rig Count Hits a Two-Year High — But Drillers Still Won't Chase $100 Oil Like They Used To

<cite index="94-17">The U.S. rig count rose by four to 595 for the week ended September 18</cite>, its best level since May 2024. Yet the composition tells the real story: <cite index="94-12">producers are still showing considerably more restraint than might historically have been expected with oil above $100</cite>, and <cite index="96-10,96-11">historically, a sustained move to triple-digit oil prices would have created strong pressure to accelerate drilling</cite>. If shale won't answer this price signal the way it used to, the market has one less release valve left for every future supply shock.
