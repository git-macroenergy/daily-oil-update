## Iran and Oman Are About to Manage the Strait of Hormuz — Without a US Signature on the Deal

Watch what happens when Washington isn't at the table. Iran's foreign ministry says a deal with Oman to formally manage Hormuz shipping is now days away, to be lodged with the IMO as an independent bilateral arrangement. <cite index="21-1">Iran said a deal with Oman to manage shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is imminent, a move that could tighten Tehran's control over the waterway and raise questions over how the US would respond.</cite> Trump has previously threatened to bomb Oman over exactly this kind of arrangement — the real test now is whether Washington accepts a chokepoint deal it didn't write.

## Goldman's Real Call Isn't $120 Oil — It's to Stop Trading Crude and Start Trading Diesel

The $120 headline is the least useful part of Goldman's note. <cite index="47-16">Goldman recommends natural gas and diesel trades to capture gains rather than relying only on crude.</cite> The reasoning: <cite index="48-5">In gas and fuels, 'the supply shocks are bigger than in the crude market,' the expert said.</cite> Crude has cushions — dark fleets, China's price sensitivity, Gulf rerouting — that diesel and gas don't. If you're hedging this war, the chokepoint to watch isn't Brent's headline number; it's the crack spread.

## Guyana Is About to Pump Past Venezuela — And Nobody's Pricing In the Barrels

While the market obsesses over Hormuz barrels, a genuinely new stream of non-OPEC, non-chokepoint supply is about to hit water. Guyana's government pegs September for Uaru's first oil; <cite index="39-14">ExxonMobil, the project's operator, has consistently guided to a fourth-quarter 2026 startup.</cite> Either way, the math is the same: <cite index="43-13">Passing 1 million barrels per day would make Guyana South America's second-largest oil producer, ahead of Venezuela and behind only Brazil.</cite> A war-premium market with a supply story nobody's modeling is a mispricing waiting to happen.
