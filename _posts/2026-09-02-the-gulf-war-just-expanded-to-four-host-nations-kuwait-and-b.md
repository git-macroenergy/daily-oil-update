## The Gulf War Just Expanded to Four Host Nations — Kuwait and Bahrain Are the New Targets, Not Just Jordan and the UAE

Watch which Gulf capitals get pulled in next, because that determines whether the two-thirds pre-war export recovery holds or unwinds. After a month of calm, US strikes on Larak Island triggered Iranian retaliation that hit not just Jordan and the UAE but reportedly Kuwait and Bahrain too — the actual crude-exporting neighbors, not just basing states. Brent has already climbed to around $95, up more than 30% since the war began. ## Diesel Margins Just Broke Their Own Record Again — Right as Refiners Enter Fall Turnaround Season at 97% Capacity

The real risk this fall isn't another Hormuz headline — it's a compressor or FCC unit failing at a refinery that's had zero slack for months. US refiners are running near 97% capacity to cover global refining losses, and one energy-finance professor's blunt read applies: you can't run that hot continually without something breaking. Right on cue, the US diesel crack spread punched to a fresh all-time high above $106 a barrel this week. Deferring planned maintenance into this margin environment raises unplanned-outage odds just as distillate cover is already thin.

## Chevron Just Confirmed the Mechanics of Trump's Venezuela Deal — $7 Billion to Double Output, But Nobody Can Say the Deal Survives the Next Administration

The number to watch isn't the $7 billion — it's whether Venezuela's acting government actually has the legal standing to make this deal stick. Chevron confirmed new acreage in the Orinoco Belt's Carabobo-1 and Carabobo-2-South-A areas, part of a plan to invest over $7 billion over five years and more than double output to roughly 600,000 barrels a day. Analysts are already flagging that reviving decades of neglected infrastructure takes years, and that a future Venezuelan or US administration could still unwind the underlying rights.
