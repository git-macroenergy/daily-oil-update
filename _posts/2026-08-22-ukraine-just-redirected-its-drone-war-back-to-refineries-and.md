## Ukraine Just Redirected Its Drone War Back to Refineries — And Rosneft's Bashneft Complex Took the Hit

Kyiv just pivoted its long-range campaign away from tankers and back to refineries, hitting Rosneft's Bashneft complex in Ufa and sparking a fire. <cite index="25-2,25-5">Ukraine has struck another oil refinery in Russia's Bashkortostan region, with the Ufa region housing three refineries owned by Bashneft, a subsidiary of Rosneft</cite>. The strike lands as <cite index="24-8">Ukraine has struck most of Russia's oil refineries in recent months, leading to acute fuel shortages in some parts of the country</cite>. Every barrel of Russian diesel that doesn't get refined domestically is one more cargo China and India have to bid for elsewhere.

## Orlen Just Locked In a Quarter of Its Crude From Norway — Europe's Quiet Hedge Against Hormuz Has a Name

While traders obsess over Hormuz tolls and Red Sea reroutes, Central Europe just quietly de-risked itself. <cite index="42-1,42-4">Poland's Orlen secured up to a quarter of its crude oil needs through a new agreement with Equinor, with the three-year contract running from September 2026 and annual supplies ranging from 5 million to over 9 million tonnes</cite>. <cite index="44-5">The crude will be supplied to ORLEN refineries in Poland, Lithuania and the Czech Republic, giving the Central European refiner a longer-term source of oil as the region continues to diversify its energy supply</cite>. Expect more term deals like this as Middle East supply risk becomes structural, not episodic.

## Dangote Isn't Just Building a Kenya Refinery — He's Handing Governments Equity So They Can't Undercut Him

Dangote's East Africa play looks less like philanthropy and more like a moat. <cite index="49-1,49-4">Aliko Dangote has offered East African countries a combined 30% equity stake in his proposed Kenya refinery, with Kenya taking 10% while Ethiopia and Rwanda have also expressed interest</cite>. It lands right as <cite index="51-1">Nigeria's Dangote Refinery has secured a $1 billion underwriting programme for its planned stock market listing that could become Africa's largest IPO</cite>. Giving regional governments a cut buys political cover for cross-border product flows — and locks out rival refiners before they even break ground.
