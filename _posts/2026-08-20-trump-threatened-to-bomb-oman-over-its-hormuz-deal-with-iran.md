## Trump Threatened to Bomb Oman Over Its Hormuz Deal With Iran — the 'Quiet Bypass' Just Got Loud

Forget a negotiated Hormuz reopening anytime soon: Trump has now threatened to bomb Oman over the very Iran-Oman transit arrangement traders were counting on to restart normal shipping. The U.S.-Iran 60-day memorandum expired without renewal, and the UAE suspended all trade and financial transactions with Iran after its air defenses detected two ballistic missiles aimed at its territory. What looked like a workaround forming without Washington a few days ago now looks like a three-way standoff where every party has a reason to torpedo it. Plan for the corridor staying contested into September, not clearing.

## US Refiners Are Skipping Turnarounds to Chase Record Margins — One Bad Trip Wipes Out the Cushion

US refinery processing rates just hit their highest level since September 2019, but underneath that number is a maintenance backlog. Motiva has delayed the turnaround on its biggest Port Arthur crude unit by a year, to fall 2027, to keep capturing strong margins, and it isn't alone in that bet. The trade-off works until it doesn't: unplanned Gulf Coast outages already averaged 170,000 b/d this spring versus 300,000 a year earlier, and distillate stocks just fell to their lowest level in over a month even as crude inventories built. There's no spare capacity left to absorb a big unplanned trip this fall.

## Iran Just Stopped Boarding Tankers and Started Just Taking Them

For months, Iran's Hormuz enforcement meant strikes, mines, and insurance surcharges. The Amara incident this week reads differently: the tanker sailed into the gulf empty with transponders on, made at least five U-turns, and went to a standstill near Qeshm Island, with Iranian media claiming the IRGC intercepted it and redirected it toward Bandar Abbas for skipping Iran's designated route and service fee. If seizure-and-redirect becomes Iran's default enforcement tool rather than the exception, owners aren't just pricing in a transit fee or a war-risk premium anymore — they're pricing in losing the ship.
